The one idea

Everything in the payoff chain is deterministic given deal terms — except one thing.

01The only random quantity is fcash, the fraction of shares that elect cash at the deadline. So the model is a single stochastic node: draw fcash → proration → optimal-election consideration → edge, repeated to build a distribution.

02In a fully-prorated deal the blended (average) consideration is fixed by the cash pool — it does not depend on demand. The arbitrage edge comes entirely from optimal election: elect the richer side, and when that side is under-subscribed you capture more of it than the average holder. How much you capture depends on the demand draw. That is what the Monte Carlo prices.

On spread conditioning

Rational holders should tilt toward the richer side, so demand ought to respond to the deadline spread. On our data that logit slope is ≈ 0 (thin, n=32). The framework supports conditioning but doesn’t assert it — it Monte-Carlos over the slope’s uncertainty rather than overfitting a curve.

Risk-aware trade layer

The signal layer prices both the long election strategy and the reverse trade under completed / terminated / withdrawn probabilities.

01ENTER buys the target, elects the higher-expected side, and shorts the expected acquirer-stock receipt. This is the election-right trade.

02REVERSE shorts the target only when the passive settlement liability is attractive after outcome risk. It has no election right, so completion payoff is the blended consideration, not the optimal elected side.

03Current blotter: 88 priced signals, 10 ENTER, 10 REVERSE, 20 REVIEW. Trade-book mean expected return is 6.4%; realized coverage mean is 6.4% with hit rate 100%. Signal/realized correlation on covered trades is +0.78.

04Capacity overlay. ENTER capacity is finite because our target shares change aggregate election demand. What’s solid, and what needs one more pull

  • Demand distribution — solid (n=73) and near the disclosure ceiling. No more material recovery expected from EDGAR; the missing labels are mostly not disclosed.
  • Calibration, realized edge, and outcome-risk overlay — done on local inputs. The current run uses event-level completed / terminated / withdrawn probabilities where available.
  • Risk-aware long/reverse signal layer — done. The reverse strategy explicitly uses passive blended settlement because the short side has no election right.
  • Capacity and election-impact overlay — done. Holder mix is now rolling-estimated from prior events, and the blotter reports raw maximum, risk-gated maximum, profit-optimal size, historical-election realized P&L, and self-impact election realized P&L.
  • Selection caveat to state plainly. The disclosers skew large/liquid — the demand distribution is “demand among deals that disclose,” which is close to the tradeable set but not the full M&A universe.
